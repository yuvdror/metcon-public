Four things the AI does for you

AI form check

Film a set from the side. The AI watches your reps and tells you what to fix, where in the rep it happens, why it matters — and the drill that corrects it. Up to a minute, no sound.

AI workouts for every way you train

MetCon, Gym, HIIT, Calisthenics, Running and Mobility. Tell it how long you have; it writes today's session from your logbook and the kit you actually own, and says which goal it moves.

AI warm-ups

For exactly what you're about to do — a coach session, a benchmark, or the workout chalked on your gym's whiteboard — with the movements and loads in it.

An AI training plan

Pick your days, what you want to train and a goal. It lays out weeks on your calendar with deloads built in, and writes each day's session on the day, from how training is really going.

It tracks your progress

Goals that match how you train — a heavier lift, a faster benchmark, a 5 km time, strict pull-ups, a longer hold, sessions a week — measured from where you started. Every session the coach writes says which goal it moved.

🧠

It trains the limiter, not the target

A sub-4 Fran is not bought by doing Fran every week. It's bought by unbroken thruster capacity and chest-to-bar cycling under fatigue. That is what lands on your whiteboard.

📈

It reads the whole logbook

Your one-rep maxes, so the loads are real numbers. Your last five scores and the RPE you left them at, so it won't stack a third redline session on a bad week. Your Rx rate per movement, so it programs the thing you keep scaling instead of politely avoiding it.

📷

No more typing in the WOD

One photo of the whiteboard and you have a timer, a log entry and a leaderboard. It shows you what it read before it saves anything — and the photo is deleted from your phone before the request even finishes.

⏱

A clock you can trust

Thruster reads the clock instead of counting ticks, so a 20-minute AMRAP is 20 minutes even with the screen off and the phone on the floor. Cues play on the alarm channel, so you hear them over your music.

🏅

PRs, compared properly

Log a score and Thruster tells you if you beat your best — matching Rx against Rx and scaled against scaled, so you never get a medal for doing less work. Hit a target and it closes itself.

Tell it what you're training for

Two taps. A number on a bar, a benchmark time, or "stop scaling this". If you can't summarise a year of training into a number on the spot, it reads your logbook and offers you three — the next plate on your heaviest lift, ten percent off a benchmark you've actually done, the movement you scale most.

Why this isn't a workout generator

The difference is what the model is given before it writes a word.

A generator knows

How long you've got. What equipment you have. Maybe your experience level, if it asked.

Thruster's coach knows

All of that, plus every target you've set and how far off each one is. Your one-rep maxes. Your last five sessions with the scores and the RPE. The share of sessions you've gone Rx on every movement in the library, and what you substituted when you didn't.

Which is why

It won't program ring muscle-ups for someone who has scaled them nine times out of ten — and it won't quietly drop them either. It programs them at a volume you can finish, and writes the scaling notes for you rather than for a generic beginner.
